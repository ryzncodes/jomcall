# Call patterns: validation and final list

- **Date:** 2026-11-14
- **Status:** Research and draft data. No product code depends on this yet.
- **Companion data:** `docs/research/pattern-packs/` (one JSON file per pattern, plus a shared pack).

This document checks the seven working call patterns against what could be found on the web, and ends with a final list. A pattern is defined by what a call is for, not by industry.

## How to read the evidence

Three things limit this research, and they shape how much weight each section can carry.

1. **Most sources are secondary.** Web research was done on 2026-11-14 by search and page fetch. Ministry of Health sites blocked automated fetching, and the text of most Acts was not opened on the Attorney General's Chambers site. Section numbers are as reported by law-firm notes, news reports and regulator pages. Each row carries a confidence label. Nothing here is legal advice; a Malaysian lawyer should review the sector rules before launch.
2. **No source was found for how customers phrase enquiries**, apart from one dental clinic's FAQ page and marketplace vocabulary. The phrasings in section 2 are written from general familiarity with Malaysian usage and are marked as such. They should be replaced with real transcripts once the owner has recordings or chat exports.
3. **No source quantifies enquiry reasons by business type, or how common mixed businesses are.** The taxonomy is therefore validated by reasoning about call purpose and tools, not by counts.

Confidence labels: **high** (primary text or official page read), **med** (reputable secondary source, or a search extract of a primary source), **low** (single weak source, vendor claim or inference).

### What they mean

Nearly nine in ten Malaysian small businesses are in services and seven in ten are micro enterprises. These are the shops where the owner is also the person who answers the phone. Social media presence is more common than a website among establishments with any web presence, which supports the earlier observation that owners keep their information in chats, social pages and photos, not on a website. The DOSM figures cover all establishments, not only small ones, so the true website share among micro enterprises is likely lower. That last point is an inference.

The businesses that depend most on phone and WhatsApp enquiries share three traits: the customer cannot buy without asking something first (is it in stock, are you free on Saturday, how much for my case); the answer changes from day to day; and the owner's hands are busy when the question arrives. That covers service counters (salons, clinics, workshops), trades that travel (aircon, plumbing, movers), made-to-order sellers (bakers, caterers, florists), rentals (homestays, halls, cars), enrolment businesses (tuition, kindergartens, gyms) and professional firms.

Two observations hold across the table. The same four question shapes recur in every pattern: price, availability, time and "what happens next". And the reason for calling is rarely the last thing said: a status call turns into approval of extra work, and a price call turns into a booking. Packs therefore need to combine cleanly.

## 3. Business types that fit no pattern, or need two

### Types that need two or more packs

| Business | Packs | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Car or motorcycle workshop | quote-visit, status-check, often stock-price | Customers ask for a repair estimate, then ask whether the car is ready, and many workshops sell parts over the counter |
| GP clinic that dispenses | appointment, with the health rules | Malaysia has no separation of prescribing and dispensing, so clinics supply medicines ([CodeBlue, 2025-08](https://codeblue.galencentre.org/2025/08/mandatory-prescription-sparks-doctors-fury-dispensing-separation-fears/), high confidence, qualitative). Medicine questions must go to the doctor, so stock-price is not added |
| Pharmacy with a consultation room | stock-price for non-prescription lines, appointment for health screening | Prescription items are excluded by rule |
| Homestay that caters, or hall with catering | reservation-rental, order | Dates and food are priced and confirmed separately |
| Restaurant | reservation-rental for tables, order for takeaway and catering | Two different capacities: seats and kitchen |
| Tailor, printing shop, optical shop | order or appointment, then status-check | Almost every made-to-order business gets "is it ready" calls |
| Tuition centre, kindergarten, gym | enrolment-lead for new enquiries; existing customers' calls go to staff | Calls about a named child or a member's account need caller verification, which does not exist yet |
| Property agent | enrolment-lead with a listings catalogue, appointment for viewings | The catalogue is the product |
| Veterinary or specialist clinic | appointment plus case-intake rules | Booking is by slot, but the advice limits are those of a regulated profession |
| Renovation contractor | quote-visit, then status-check for the project | A long project is one job with many stages |

No evidence was found on how common each mixed type is. The table is reasoning from call purpose.

### Types that fit poorly

| Business | Why it fits poorly | Suggested handling |
|---|---|---|
| Goldsmith, money changer, pawnbroker | The price changes every day or hour; a wrong figure costs money at once | stock-price with a price that expires the same day, or no prices by phone at all. Pawnbroking and moneylending also carry advertising rules |
| Insurance and takaful agent, financial planner, licensed moneylender | Almost everything a caller wants is advice or a recommendation, which needs a licence | case-intake only: take details, book the licensed person. Low value for JomCall until reviewed by a lawyer |
| Funeral and burial services | Urgent at any hour, emotionally heavy, and religion-specific | Shared pack with an immediate handoff. Not a good first market for an AI voice |
| Wholesaler and distributor | Per-customer prices, credit terms, standing orders | stock-price for the public list only; trade customers go to staff |
| Kedai runcit and food stalls | Few calls; customers walk in | Not a target |
| Runner and agent services (road tax, permits, visa) | The customer's question is "what do I need", which is fact lookup plus status-check | every-business plus status-check; no new pack |
| Delivery-led businesses (gas, water, lorry) | The order is simple but the question is "where is my driver" | order plus status-check; needs a live delivery status, which is a data gap |
| Schools and childcare, calls from parents | The caller wants facts about a child | Refuse by rule and take a message until callers can be verified |
| Emergency trades (towing, locksmith, 24-hour plumber) | The call is a dispatch, not an enquiry | quote-visit with the urgent rule; needs a real call transfer, which is a tool gap |

### Halal

Only JAKIM and the state religious authorities certify halal. Under the Trade Descriptions (Definition of Halal) Order 2011 and the Certification and Marking Order 2011, a false halal description carries fines reported at up to RM 5 million for a company ([Malay Mail, 2025-01-16](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2025/01/16/misuse-of-halal-logo-as-ham-sandwich-probe-sparks-calls-for-higher-penalties-armizan-says-fines-of-up-to-rm5m-prison-terms-already-severe/163478), med). Certification remains voluntary ([Daily Express, 2026-07-02](https://www.dailyexpress.com.my/news/245926/), med). Phrases such as "no pork no lard" are not clearly an offence nationally, but the Selangor religious department says uncertified outlets may not use them ([The Sun, 2025-08-18](https://thesun.my/viral/going-viral/jais-prohibits-no-pork-no-lard-signs-for-uncertified-shops-DC14709192), med).

For the agent: say "halal" only when the owner has recorded a valid certificate with its issuer and expiry date. Otherwise repeat the owner's factual statement word for word and add nothing. This rule sits in the shared pack because callers ask it of salons, caterers, homestays and bakeries alike.

### Children

Under the Personal Data Protection Act 2010, consent for a person under 18 comes from a parent or guardian (med). Tuition centres and kindergartens register with the Ministry of Education and nurseries with the Social Welfare Department (med). **No statute was found** that says what a centre may confirm by phone about a child. The rule in the enrolment pack, which is never to confirm enrolment, attendance or collection to a caller and never to accept a change of who collects a child, is a safety judgement, not a legal finding. It is the right default until callers can be verified against the number on file.

## 5. Conclusion: keep, split, merge or add

### Final list: eight patterns and one shared pack

| # | Pack | Change from the seven | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `every-business` | Shared, as planned | Hours, location, payment, handoff, emergencies and the halal rule apply to everyone |
| 1 | `appointment` | **Keep** | Clear purpose; the only pattern the existing tools serve end to end |
| 2 | `stock-price` | **Keep** | Depends on a searched catalogue; no other pattern does so heavily |
| 3 | `quote-visit` | **Keep** | The job is at the customer's place and the price needs a person; lead capture is the product |
| 4 | `order` | **Split** from "order and reservation" | An order is a list of goods for a date, limited by kitchen capacity and lead time. It needs a menu catalogue and an order record |
| 5 | `reservation-rental` | **Split** from "order and reservation" | A reservation holds a unit for a date range, limited by inventory. It needs range availability and date-based rates. The facts, rules and tools barely overlap with an order |
| 6 | `enrolment-lead` | **Keep** | The call is a slow decision; the business wants a qualified lead and a visit |
| 7 | `case-intake` | **Rename and narrow** "professional" | "Professional" named an industry, not a purpose. The purpose is intake: record the matter, check urgency, book the professional, give no advice. Specialist and veterinary clinics move to `appointment` and borrow these rules |
| 8 | `status-check` | **Keep**, as a secondary pack | Rarely a business's only pattern, and it is the one with no working tool today |

### Why not merge

- **Appointment and reservation** look alike, since both check a calendar. They differ in what is held (a person's time against a set of nights or a vehicle), in pricing (per service against per date tier) and in the tool needed (one start time against a date range).
- **Stock-price and order** both end in a sale. Stock-price answers "do you have it now"; order answers "can you make it by then". Florists and bakers were listed under stock-price and belong under order.
- **Quote-visit and enrolment-lead** both capture a lead. One collects an address and a fault; the other collects a person's level and intent, and carries the children's and outcome-promise rules.

### What to add that is not a pattern

1. **Sector guards.** The rules in section 4 belong to a sector, not to a call purpose: a dental clinic and a hair salon both use `appointment`, but only one must refuse to discuss medicines. Each pack already carries the guards most likely to apply. The next step is to pull them into small rule-only overlays (health, pharmacy, legal and tax, financial, property, children, food) that the business-type picker switches on. Rules outrank everything, so an overlay is safe to add to any pack.
2. **A complaint and after-sales path.** Every pattern receives complaints, refund requests and warranty claims. They are handled in the shared pack as an immediate handoff. This is not a pattern because the agent's job is the same everywhere: record and pass on.
3. **Existing-customer calls.** Calls about a named child, a member's account, a client's file or another person's repair all need the caller to be verified against a number on file. Until that exists, every pack refuses and takes a message.

### Gaps that decide what can ship

| Gap | Packs blocked | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Catalogue with structured search (price as a number, other names, fitment, stock note) and import from a photo, PDF, Excel or WhatsApp catalogue | stock-price, order, reservation-rental, enrolment-lead | The largest single gap. Today's knowledge items are free text of three kinds |
| Job list with status lookup, and a fast way for staff to update it | status-check | Without it the pack can only take messages |
| Date-range availability, range reservation and a date-based quote | reservation-rental | The current tools book one start time on one resource for up to seven days ahead |
| Structured lead, order and intake records | quote-visit, order, enrolment-lead, case-intake | `take_message` holds one free-text field |
| Real call transfer and urgent paging | all, most of all quote-visit and case-intake | `transfer_call` is in the design spec but not in the tool contract |
| Find, move and cancel a booking | appointment | Staff handle changes today |
| Caller verification against the number on file | status-check, enrolment-lead, case-intake, appointment | Needed before any record is read out |
| WhatsApp photo intake | stock-price, quote-visit, order | Owners and customers both think in photos |
| State public-holiday calendar | every-business | In the spec; dates must be maintained yearly |
| Deposit payment link | appointment, order, reservation-rental | Waits on the payment provider decision |
| Sensitive-data handling and the cross-border decision | case-intake, appointment in health settings | Open in the handoff notes |

### Suggested order

1. `every-business` and `appointment` first: they work with today's tools.
2. `quote-visit`, `enrolment-lead` and `case-intake` next: they deliver value as careful message-taking and need only a structured record.
3. `stock-price` and `order` after the catalogue exists.
4. `reservation-rental` and `status-check` last: each needs its own data and tools.
